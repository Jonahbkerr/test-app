    Why perception might not be reality

    1. There is no unfiltered door

    Every route starts at the senses. Before you can think about the world, a narrow set of nerve endings has already decided which small slice of it reaches you.

    2. The order changes the picture

    Doubt before a rumor can't catch the rumor. Doubt after it can. The same filters in a different order produce a different reality, and none of them feels like a filter from the inside.

    3. Arriving feels like seeing

    At the exit every traveler feels they're looking at what's really there. The brain hides its own editing, so the result shows up as plain fact and not as a guess.

    The filters in the maze

    The map is not the territory

    What we call "reality" is a model the brain builds from limited signals, shaped by attention, memory, expectation, mood, words, culture and other people. Good models are very useful. That's why we survive. But they are still models.

    The practical lesson: when someone else's "reality" disagrees with yours, they may simply have taken a different route through the maze. Comparing routes, using instruments, and doubting yourself in the right order gets you closer, but you never step outside the maze.
